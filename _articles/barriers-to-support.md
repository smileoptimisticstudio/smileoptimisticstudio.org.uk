When does a necessary check become a barrier to support? I understand why disability benefit systems need careful assessments. Public money must be managed responsibly. Decisions need evidence.

There must be clear rules. I don't question that. But after months of navigating the PIP process for my daughter, I've started thinking about something else. Every additional form, phone call, request for evidence or reconsideration takes time and energy.

For a healthy person, this may be frustrating. For someone living with severe fatigue, chronic illness or depression, it may be overwhelming. I wonder whether we sometimes measure a person's ability to navigate bureaucracy rather than their actual need for support. Surely, protecting public resources and making support accessible should not be competing priorities.

A system can be thorough without making the people who need it most feel they must prove themselves endlessly. Perhaps the real question is not whether we need checks, but how we design them around the people they are meant to serve.
