When someone finishes cancer treatment, it is natural to celebrate. So much courage, care and effort has gone into reaching that moment. The relief is real, and so is the gratitude.

But the end of treatment does not always mean the end of its impact. Life afterwards may involve rebuilding confidence, finding a new rhythm, navigating uncertainty and discovering what independence looks like now.

We often measure medical success through treatment outcomes. Those measures matter enormously. Yet quality of life asks different questions. Can a young person return to the things they enjoy? Do they feel heard? Do they have opportunities to make choices, connect with friends and imagine a future of their own?

As a parent, I have learnt that survival and recovery are not the same story. The years after treatment deserve attention, understanding and support in their own right.

Surviving is an extraordinary milestone. Supporting someone to live fully afterwards matters too.
