There is a psychological term that describes one of the hardest tasks a parent faces in hospital: **emotional containment**.

It does not mean calming the child down. It means being able to hold a child's fear, anger or despair without denying it — and without demanding:

"Don't cry." "Don't be scared." "Be brave."

Sometimes it is enough to say: *"I can see you're frightened. I'm here."*

The hardest part is that, in that moment, the parent is often just as frightened.

That is why children and adults in hospital need simple tools that help them get through these moments together.

One aim of the Hospital Joy Toolkit is to give the adult something ready to lean on: words, a simple action, a way to lower the demand, or permission to stop.

Not to replace a psychologist — but to help when a child and parent are left with a hard feeling, right now.
