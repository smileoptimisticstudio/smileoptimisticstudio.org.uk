Sometimes one word tells an entire story.

I remember quiet conversations with other parents in hospital corridors. Often, we did not need to explain very much. A glance, a brief question or a few words could carry a whole day of worry, hope and exhaustion.

Outside that shared experience, the same words could sound ordinary. Inside it, they held the weight of countless appointments, difficult choices and hours spent waiting.

Years later, I still think about the distance between what professionals can record and what a family experiences. A form may capture an event. A report may describe a situation. Neither can always convey what it feels like to live through it, day after day.

That is why I believe listening matters as much as asking the right questions. Not every important detail fits neatly into a box. Sometimes the most meaningful part of a story is the part that has not yet been said.

Behind every brief answer, there may be a whole life waiting to be understood.
