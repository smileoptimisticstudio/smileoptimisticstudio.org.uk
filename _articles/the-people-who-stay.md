I talk a lot about the Hospital Joy Toolkit — the cards, the methodology, the research, the professional review.

But the studio's main resource is not the cards. It is people: people who can work with the subject of children with cancer and not look away.

Once, I was simply looking for someone to help edit a book — light, sometimes funny stories about children in treatment. It seemed like just a text. But people started and then left. Some at the very beginning, some halfway through. Not because they were poor professionals. The subject was too heavy.

I saw something else, too: people stopped talking to me because it hurt them even to watch our story.

That taught me something important. Professional qualifications are not enough for work in this field. It also takes the inner strength to stay close.

So the studio's greatest asset is people who bring knowledge, empathy and enough strength not to run from a difficult subject.

Cards can always be reprinted. People like that cannot.
