What if medical evidence and disability assessments spoke the same language? I've learnt something important while navigating the PIP process for my daughter. A medical diagnosis alone doesn't determine eligibility for disability support. PIP assesses how a person's condition affects specific daily activities and mobility.

That makes sense. But here's the difficulty. A hospital report might describe a medical history and long-term effects of treatment in great detail. It may say very little about whether that person can prepare a meal, wash independently or manage everyday activities reliably.

So families are often left trying to translate complex medical evidence into descriptions of daily life. What if medical reports intended for disability assessments included a short section on functional limitations? Not just what condition does this person have? But what does this condition prevent them from doing, and what help do they need?

Doctors shouldn't have to decide who qualifies for benefits. But perhaps better communication between healthcare and social support could reduce the burden on families. Sometimes the missing evidence isn't another medical test. It's the connection between a diagnosis and everyday life.
